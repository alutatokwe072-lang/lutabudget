Budgeting

How I Plan My Money as a Student on a Tight Budget

I am a student and I get a set amount of money each month. This is how I would use it so that some of it is left over for saving. It is what works for me, and your situation may be different.

1. I start with my real number

My monthly money is R1,710. Before I spend anything, I write that number down, because every other decision depends on it.

2. I split it before I spend it

I use a simple 50/30/20 split: about R855 for needs, R513 for wants and R342 for savings. If my needs take more than half, I adjust the split, but I still set something aside.

3. I put the savings aside first

I move the savings amount away from my spending money straight away, so I am not tempted to use it. Whatever is left is what I can spend for the rest of the month.

4. I keep a close eye on small spending

Small purchases add up quickly on a small budget. Checking where my money went each week shows me what I can cut next month.

Budgeting

The 50/30/20 Rule for Students, Explained

The 50/30/20 rule splits money into 50% for needs, 30% for wants and 20% for savings. For many students needs take up more, so a split closer to 65/15/20 is common, and that is fine.

How I would split my R1,710

On R1,710 a month, the plain 50/30/20 split gives me R855 for needs, R513 for wants and R342 for savings. I treat it as a starting point and adjust it to what I actually need.

If money arrives as one lump sum

When money has to last a long stretch, I divide each bucket by the number of weeks it needs to cover, so I know what I can spend each week.
