About LutaBudget

LutaBudget started out of necessity — a way of keeping track of money that never seemed to stretch quite as far as it needed to. That habit grew into a resource for other South African students figuring out the same thing: how to budget, save, and understand money without a financial background to lean on.

LutaBudget isn't a bank, and it doesn't offer financial products. It's a plain-language guide to budgeting and saving, written for real student life.

How to Save Money as a Student on a Tight Budget

When your income is small and irregular, "just save 20%" can feel like advice from a different planet.

1. Know your real number

Track two weeks of spending before deciding on a plan.

2. Give every rand a job

Split income the moment it lands, savings first.

3. Automate the saving

A standing order into a separate account beats daily willpower.

Budgeting

The 50/30/20 Rule for Students, Explained

Split income into 50% needs, 30% wants, and 20% savings. For many students needs take up more — a split closer to 65/15/20 is common, and that's fine.

Applying it to irregular income

If money arrives as one lump sum, divide each bucket by the number of weeks it needs to last.

See this applied to a real NSFAS allowance example in the budget calculator.
